Who I am

About Me

I'm a Ph.D. student in Mechanical Engineering at Villanova University, working in the Laboratory for Advanced Thermal and Fluid Systems. My research focuses on Flow Network Modeling, thermal-fluid system analysis, experimental validation, and Digital Twin development for large-scale, high-power data center cooling systems.

My current work spans two-phase cooling component modeling in Flownex, system-level integration of vendor hardware and experimental data, and interactive Digital Twin development in NVIDIA Omniverse.

Selected work

Projects

OMNICOOL — 1 MW-Class Two-Phase Data Center Cooling System (ARPA-E)

A 250 kW self-contained, containerized POD with a clear scaling path to 1 MW+, demonstrated live at the ARPA-E Energy Innovation Summit. End-to-end two-phase pumped refrigeration (R515B heat rejection / R1234yf at the chip) achieves ~0.005°C/W package thermal resistance, PUE below 1.05, and zero water consumption at ≥40°C ambient. Built with Vertiv, Boyd (Eaton), Binghamton University, and Villanova University, integrated with a physics-based NVIDIA Omniverse Digital Twin.

ARPA-ETwo-Phase RefrigerationNVIDIA OmniverseDigital Twin

Two-Phase Data Center Component Library

Cold-plates, quick disconnects, flow regulators, and two-phase heat exchangers developed in the Flownex Simulation Environment and validated against experimental test data (e.g. Kandlikar two-phase cold-plate model).

FlownexTwo-Phase CoolingValidation

Single-Phase vs. Two-Phase Cooling Comparison

Transient simulation of identical server racks under a 40%→100% heat-load spike; the two-phase system showed a 30% lower chip case-temperature rise (29.5°C vs. 42.1°C) due to latent-heat cooling.
